---
title: "Claude Code, Codex, and Staying Inside the Frame"
date: "Mid-July 2026"
description: "Initiative can feel magical, but engineering work also needs an agent that knows how to stay inside the frame."
---

I tried Claude Code for a quick prototype last week, and I now understand why non-technical people love it.

The UX is genuinely impressive. There is a kind of momentum to it that keeps you smiling with each turn in the session. It takes initiative in ways that can feel magical. Sometimes it looks at the task, sees the gaps, and fills them before you even ask.

But that is also where the problem starts.

Sometimes it fixes things that did not need fixing.

Sometimes it touches files it was not supposed to touch.

Sometimes it goes beyond the instruction in a way that feels helpful at first, until you realize you now have to review a much larger surface area than you intended.

For someone who does not know exactly what they want, that initiative can be useful.

For a technical user, it can be frustrating.

This is why I still find myself sticking with Codex for engineering work.

Its coherence over long contexts has been superb for me. More importantly, it does not keep going on side quests. It does what I asked it to do. And if it notices something odd, it does not silently “improve” half the codebase.

It comes back and says something like:

> “Done. By the way, I noticed XYZ while performing the task.”

That is the kind of predictability I want as an engineer.

I do not just want an agent that can move fast.

I want one that knows how to stay inside the frame.
