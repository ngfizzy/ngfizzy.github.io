---
title: "Monday check-in: the 5 persons unicorn"
date: 2026-08-25
description: "The one-person unicorn may be possible, but five people feels more believable."
---

I have been thinking about this idea of the one-person unicorn.

I am an early adopter of technology. I like being on the bleeding edge, and I adopted this agentic engineering thing pretty early.

Here is what I can tell you from using it seriously:

I am not buying the one-person unicorn yet.

Maybe three people. Five feels very believable.

My version of the five-person company looks something like this:

A CTO, a marketer, a legal person, an accountant, and someone to play the Sean Parker role. All domain experts in their respective fields, internet-savvy and AI-native.

Kinda like the Silicon Valley comedy setup.

But most importantly, you need attention.

This is the part I think gets lost in the conversation about 10x engineers and autonomous agents.

The people I know who are getting ridiculous amounts of leverage from these tools are not casually typing a prompt into ChatGPT a few times a day.

They are spending a lot of time with them. Running multiple agents, reviewing output, correcting mistakes, giving more context, restarting things that went in the wrong direction and generally keeping an eye on a lot of moving pieces.

The bottleneck starts moving from "can I do this?" to "how many things can I pay attention to at once?"

And then there is cost.

I know someone who personally got to around $2,000 a month in AI usage and had to start watching it.

Uber reportedly had a similar problem at a much bigger scale, with employees burning through what was supposed to be a much longer AI budget in only a few months.

So when I imagine a tiny company where agents are doing the work of tens or hundreds of people, I also imagine a surprisingly large compute bill sitting somewhere behind it.

The marginal cost of getting more work done may be collapsing, but it is not zero.

This is why five people feels much more believable to me than one.

Not because the AI cannot do enough.

But because somebody still has to decide what matters, direct all of it, inspect the output, talk to people, build trust, make judgment calls and keep the whole thing moving in roughly the same direction.

Maybe the one-person unicorn happens.

But just like many of my past opinions about AI, I look forward to changing my mind.
