title: "Monday Check-in: The AI Bubble; The Other Side of the Economics"
date: 2026-08-31
description: "AI may intensify global labour competition, not just competition between developers and AI."
---

A lot of software engineers in the West seem to think they are competing with AI in the current job market.

I think that framing is incomplete.

Many developers in the US and Europe now spend somewhere around €100 to €200 a month across AI subscriptions.

That is roughly ₦150,000 to ₦300,000 (Nigerian naira).

For some perspective, a federal Permanent Secretary sits at the very top of the Nigerian civil service salary structure.

One [salary breakdown](https://infosteps.com.ng/nigerian-federal-civil-service-salary-structure/) puts the monthly basic salary around ₦576,000 (Nigerian naira), rising with seniority and allowances.

And just this month, [Vanguard reported](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/08/my-salary-is-%e2%82%a6503000-permanent-secretaries-earn-%e2%82%a6900000-oborevwori/) that Delta State Governor Sheriff Oborevwori said Permanent Secretaries in the state earn about ₦900,000 (Nigerian naira) a month.

So a Western software engineer can easily spend the equivalent of a significant chunk of a very senior Nigerian civil servant’s monthly salary just on AI subscriptions.

That is the part of the AI job conversation I think is often missed.

**It's AI and ...**

...countries like Nigeria and India, where engineers have access to increasingly capable AI tools while operating under a completely different cost structure.

And access to that labour is becoming easier too.

There are agencies built around connecting Western companies with talent in these markets, while companies like [Deel](https://www.deel.com/) increasingly abstract away payroll, tax, paperwork and compliance involved in hiring across borders.

Someone earning a fraction of a Western software salary may happily take access to ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot as a serious work benefit.

And this matters beyond software jobs.

**Inference is expensive.**

If capable humans somewhere in Africa or India can perform the same work, using the same AI tools, at a fraction of the cost, that complicates the economics of the whole AI revolution.

So when someone in the West gets laid off, I would be careful about immediately attributing it to AI.

Sometimes the work is automated.

Sometimes the role genuinely disappears.

But sometimes the work simply moves somewhere cheaper, and AI makes that transition easier.

I think the conversation around developer jobs needs a wider view of what the competition actually looks like.

Sometimes it is the model.

Sometimes it is another engineer using the same model from a completely different economic reality.

And that’s Monday Check-in.
