Friday Checkout: Cut Through the Noise

Agentic loops are familiar programming ideas with an LLM in the middle.

Things are changing very fast.

And a lot of those changes do not matter.

Two years ago, people focused heavily on prompt engineering. The dominant narrative was that if you did not prompt an agent well, you would get a sloppy result.

This year, agents can make sense of your most incoherent thoughts. They can even help you clarify your own thinking by stopping to ask questions.

That is how quickly the ground shifts.

The problem is that it is easy to get overwhelmed by the noise.

And when you are overwhelmed, one of two things usually happens: you freeze and take no action, or you make rash decisions because you feel like you are already behind.

A few weeks ago, popular voices on Twitter started pushing another idea:

Prompting is outdated. You should be building agentic loops.

At first, that sounds complicated.

But when you stop and inspect what is actually being said, the hype falls away.

You are a programmer. You already know what a loop is.

A loop runs until a stop condition is met.

So the idea that you are no longer prompting an agent is not really true. An agentic loop simply runs a prompt, evaluates the result, and repeats the process until a goal is reached or a condition tells it to stop.

Designing that loop is not some mysterious new discipline either.

The more technically experienced you are, the more creative you can be with the tools, conditions, retries, checks, and feedback you place around it.

We are not leaving computer programming behind.

We are reinventing it, with an LLM in the middle.

And because the language keeps changing, it is important not to confuse new vocabulary with entirely new ideas.

To close, I will leave you with the first line of my favourite poem, Desiderata:

“Go placidly amid the noise and haste, and remember what peace there may be in silence.”

And that is Friday Checkout.

Enjoy your weekend.
